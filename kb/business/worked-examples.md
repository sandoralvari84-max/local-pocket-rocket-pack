# Business — worked examples (mentor-curated, round 3: reasoning by example)

Method: restate → retrieve facts → reason in steps → check → concise answer. General education, not advice.

## Example 1 — "A competitor undercut us. Should we cut our price to match?"
- Restate: decide whether to cut price in response to a competitor.
- Facts (advanced.md › Microeconomics, Strategy): price elasticity; margin impact; price wars; value-based pricing; differentiation.
- Steps:
  1. Check margin maths: how much extra volume must a price cut bring just to hold gross profit? (break-even on the cut.) Low margins make cuts dangerous.
  2. Estimate elasticity: will demand actually rise enough, or is the buyer choosing on value, not price?
  3. A matched cut can start a price war that erodes everyone's margin; consider defending on value/differentiation instead (bundle, service, positioning).
- Check: weighs margin + elasticity + competitive response, not a reflex match; matches the KB.
- Answer: Usually no reflexive match. Do the margin break-even and judge elasticity; if buyers value more than price, defend with differentiation. Cut only if the volume genuinely covers the lost margin and won't trigger a ruinous price war.

## Example 2 — "Is this project worth the investment?"
- Restate: decide whether to fund a project financially.
- Facts (advanced.md › Corporate finance): time value of money, NPV, opportunity cost, sunk costs.
- Steps:
  1. Estimate the future cash flows and discount them to present value (money later is worth less than money now).
  2. Net present value = discounted inflows − investment. Invest if NPV > 0 at your cost of capital.
  3. Compare against the next best use of the money (opportunity cost). Ignore money already spent (sunk cost).
- Check: decision rule (NPV > 0, opportunity cost, ignore sunk) applied correctly; matches the KB.
- Answer: Discount the expected cash flows to present value; fund it if NPV is positive at your cost of capital and it beats the next best alternative. Don't let money already spent sway the decision.
