# Personal finance — specialist layer (evidence, corrected myths) — NOT financial advice

General financial education, not personalised advice. Specifics (tax, accounts) vary by country. Consult a licensed
professional for your situation.

## The evidence most people ignore
- **Most active funds underperform** their index after fees over the long run (S&P SPIVA data, year after year).
  Fees compound against you: 1%/year can cost a large fraction of a lifetime portfolio. Low-cost broad index funds are
  the evidence-based default for most investors.
- **Past performance does not predict future returns** — that regulatory boilerplate reflects real data. Last year's
  top fund/sector is not next year's.
- Diversification reduces **unsystematic** (single-asset) risk, not **systematic** (whole-market) risk. You can't
  diversify away a market crash.

## Facts stated precisely
- **Compound interest**: rule of 72 → years to double ≈ 72 ÷ %return. Judge returns in **real (inflation-adjusted)**,
  not nominal, terms.
- **Lump-sum vs dollar-cost averaging**: investing a lump sum immediately has a **higher expected return** than
  spreading it out (markets rise more often than not); DCA mainly reduces regret/timing risk, not expected loss.
- **Sequence-of-returns risk**: in retirement, poor returns early (while withdrawing) can ruin a portfolio that would
  have survived the same returns in a different order. The "4% rule" is a rough guideline, not a guarantee.
- Risk and expected return are linked. **"Guaranteed high returns" is the signature of a scam** (Ponzi, pump-and-dump).

## Myths and nuance
- **"Always pay off debt before investing"** — true for high-interest debt (a 20% card is a guaranteed 20% return);
  for low-interest debt it's a trade-off vs expected market return and employer-match (free money first).
- **"Renting is throwing money away"** — depends on price-to-rent, time horizon, and opportunity cost of the deposit;
  owning has large hidden costs (maintenance, transaction, illiquidity).
- Emergency fund (≈3–6 months essentials) before investing; insure large, unlikely risks; keep money you need soon out
  of volatile assets.
