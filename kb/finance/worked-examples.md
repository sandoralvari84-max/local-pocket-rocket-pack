# Finance — worked examples (mentor-curated, round 3: reasoning by example)

Method: restate → retrieve facts → reason in steps → check → concise answer. General financial education — NOT personalised investment/financial advice. Consult a licensed professional for your own decisions.

## Example 1 — "Should I pay off a 20% credit card or invest the money instead?"
- Restate: compare paying down high-interest debt vs investing, in general terms.
- Facts (foundations › Debt & interest, Investing; advanced.md › risk): pay high-interest debt first; returns are uncertain and risk-linked; compounding cuts both ways.
- Steps:
  1. Paying off a 20% card is a guaranteed ~20% return (interest you no longer pay), risk-free and tax-free.
  2. Market returns are uncertain and historically well below 20% on average, with real risk of loss.
  3. A guaranteed 20% beats an uncertain lower expected return, so high-interest debt generally comes first (keep any essential emergency buffer).
- Check: compares guaranteed vs expected/uncertain returns correctly; matches the KB; not personalised.
- Answer (general): Clearing 20% debt is a guaranteed 20% return that almost no investment reliably beats, so it usually comes first. This is general education, not advice for your situation.

## Example 2 — "A fund returned 30% last year. Should I put my money in?"
- Restate: judge an investment by last year's return.
- Facts (advanced.md › Markets, Behavioural finance): past performance ≠ future; fees; diversification; recency bias; EMH.
- Steps:
  1. One year of 30% says little about the future — it may reflect risk taken or luck (recency bias).
  2. Check fees (they compound against you), the risk/volatility, and how it fits a diversified allocation.
  3. Chasing last year's winner is a classic behavioural trap; markets are hard to beat consistently after costs.
- Check: resists recency bias, raises fees/risk/diversification; matches the KB; not personalised.
- Answer (general): Last year's 30% is not a reason on its own — past performance doesn't predict future returns. Weigh fees, risk and fit within a diversified portfolio, and beware chasing winners. General education, not advice.
