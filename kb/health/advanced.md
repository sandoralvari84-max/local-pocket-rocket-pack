# Health & medicine — advanced / academic layer (mentor-curated, round 2)

Builds on health-fundamentals.md. General, evidence-based education. NOT medical advice; does not diagnose or treat. See a qualified clinician for any personal concern; call emergency services in an emergency.

## Evidence-based medicine (how to judge health claims)
- Study-design hierarchy (strongest first): systematic reviews/meta-analyses → randomised controlled trials → cohort → case-control → cross-sectional → case reports → expert opinion.
- RCTs reduce bias via randomisation, blinding and controls; observational studies show association, not proof of cause.
- Read risk carefully: absolute vs relative risk, number needed to treat (NNT), confidence intervals; beware surrogate endpoints and small samples.
- Bias & confounding; publication bias; correlation ≠ causation (Bradford Hill criteria help weigh causality).

## Epidemiology
- Incidence vs prevalence; test performance — sensitivity, specificity, and how positive predictive value falls when a condition is rare (base rates).
- Screening principles (Wilson–Jungner): screen only when the condition is important, the test is good, and early treatment helps.

## Physiology (systems overview)
- Homeostasis and feedback loops; cardiovascular (perfusion, blood pressure), respiratory (gas exchange), renal (fluid/electrolyte balance), endocrine (hormonal regulation, e.g. insulin/glucose), nervous and immune systems.

## Nutrition science
- Energy balance (intake vs expenditure) drives weight; macronutrient roles; micronutrient deficiencies; fibre and the microbiome.
- Judge diets by evidence, not fashion; whole-food, largely plant-rich patterns have the strongest long-term support.

## Pharmacology & mental health (basics)
- Pharmacokinetics (absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion) and pharmacodynamics; dose, interactions, adverse effects.
- Mental health: biopsychosocial model; evidence-based treatments (e.g. CBT, medication) exist and work; reducing stigma and early help improve outcomes.
