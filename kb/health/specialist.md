# Health — specialist layer (evidence literacy, corrected myths) — NOT medical advice

General, evidence-based education. Not diagnosis or treatment. See a clinician for personal concerns; emergencies →
emergency services.

## Reading health claims (the core skill)
- **Relative vs absolute risk**: "cuts risk 50%" may be a drop from 2% to 1% (1 point absolute) — trivial. Always ask
  for the absolute numbers and the **number needed to treat (NNT)**.
- **Evidence hierarchy**: systematic reviews/meta-analyses > RCTs > cohort > case-control > case reports > opinion.
  Most **nutrition** findings are observational (weak, confounded) — "food X causes/prevents Y" headlines rarely prove cause.
- A p-value is not the chance the claim is true; watch publication bias and tiny samples. Correlation ≠ causation.
- **Screening maths**: a highly "accurate" test for a rare condition yields mostly false positives (low positive
  predictive value). Sensitivity/specificity ≠ "chance you have it."

## Myths worth correcting (well-established)
- **"Detox" cleanses are unnecessary** — the liver and kidneys do it; no juice/supplement is needed or proven.
- **"8 glasses of water a day"** has no strong evidence; needs vary with body, activity, climate, and food water content.
- **Antibiotics don't treat viral** infections (colds, flu, most sore throats); misuse drives resistance.
- **Supplements are largely unregulated** (US: not FDA-approved for efficacy/purity); most don't help people who eat
  adequately, and some interact with medication. "Natural" ≠ safe or effective.
- **BMI** is a population screening tool, not an individual diagnosis — it misclassifies muscular and some body types.
- "Cracking knuckles causes arthritis," "sugar makes kids hyperactive," and "we use 10% of our brain" are myths.

## Mental health
- Biopsychosocial model; conditions like anxiety and depression are common and **treatable** (evidence-based: CBT and
  other therapies, medication). Early help and reducing stigma improve outcomes. Warning signs of crisis warrant
  prompt professional help.
