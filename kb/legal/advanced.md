# Law — advanced / academic layer (mentor-curated, round 2)

Builds on legal-101.md. General legal education only. NOT legal advice; laws differ by jurisdiction and change — consult a qualified lawyer for any real matter.

## Sources & systems of law
- Common law (precedent/case law, e.g. UK, US) vs civil law (codified statutes, e.g. most of Europe).
- Hierarchy: constitution → statutes → regulations → case law; public law (state–citizen) vs private law (between parties).

## Contract law (depth)
- Formation: offer, acceptance, consideration, intention, capacity, legality; certainty of terms.
- Terms: conditions vs warranties vs innominate; express vs implied; exclusion/limitation clauses (subject to statutory controls).
- Vitiating factors: misrepresentation, mistake, duress, undue influence, illegality.
- Breach & remedies: damages (expectation measure, remoteness, mitigation, liquidated vs penalty), specific performance, rescission.

## Tort / civil wrongs
- Negligence: duty of care, breach (reasonable-person standard), causation (factual "but for" + legal/proximate), damage; defences (contributory negligence, consent).
- Strict liability; vicarious liability (employer for employee); product liability.

## Intellectual property (depth)
- Copyright: subsists automatically in original works; economic + moral rights; limited term; fair use (US) / fair dealing (UK/EU) exceptions.
- Patents: novelty, inventive step (non-obviousness), industrial applicability; claims define scope; limited monopoly in exchange for disclosure.
- Trademarks: distinctiveness, classes, infringement and dilution; passing off. Trade secrets: protected while confidential.

## Data protection & liability
- GDPR: controller vs processor, lawful bases, DPIAs for high-risk processing, cross-border transfer mechanisms, breach notification, large fines. CCPA/CPRA analogues in the US.
- Corporate forms and the "corporate veil"; directors' duties; when limited liability may be pierced.

## Dispute resolution
- Litigation vs ADR (arbitration, mediation); burden/standard of proof (civil: balance of probabilities; criminal: beyond reasonable doubt).
