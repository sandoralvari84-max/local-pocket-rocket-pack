# Marketing — expert reasoning layer (how a marketing expert thinks)

Marketing reasoning starts from the customer's problem and the economics of acquiring them, not from clever copy. The skill
is matching message to the stage of awareness, and spending only where a unit actually pays back.

## Positioning method (before any campaign)
- Start from the customer and the job: who is this for, what do they want done, and what do they use now? Positioning is a choice of who NOT to serve as much as who to serve.
- Lead with the benefit (the change in the customer's life), supported by the feature, not the feature alone. People buy the outcome.
- Differentiate on something that matters to the buyer and is hard to copy; "better and cheaper" with no reason-to-believe persuades no one.
- Match the message to awareness stage: unaware audiences need the problem named; product-aware ones need proof and comparison. The same ad fails both.

## Channel economics method
- Judge a channel by CAC vs LTV, with a payback period you can finance — not by vanity reach. A channel that acquires below value and pays back fast can scale; one that does not, cannot, however many impressions it buys.
- Attribution is hard and usually flattering to the last click; triangulate (holdout tests, incrementality) rather than trusting the dashboard's story.
- Test small, read the result honestly, then scale the winner. Most of the budget should chase proven unit economics, a slice should explore.

### Worked case — "our ads get tons of clicks but few sales"
- Separate the funnel stages: clicks measure the ad; sales measure the whole path. A click/sale gap points downstream (landing page, offer, price, or audience mismatch), not to "more clicks".
- Check message-match: does the landing page deliver what the ad promised, for the awareness stage of who clicked? A mismatch leaks the whole click.
- Check the unit economics: even if you fix conversion, does a customer earn more than they cost to acquire? If not, scaling the ad scales the loss.
- Decide with a cheap test (fix one thing — offer, page, or audience) and read conversion, not clicks.

## The meta-rule
An expert ties every tactic back to a number that matters (CAC, LTV, payback, incremental revenue) and states the test that
would prove it wrong. Creativity in service of economics; a campaign you cannot measure is a guess you cannot defend.
