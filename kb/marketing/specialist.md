# Marketing — specialist layer (evidence that contradicts common advice)

The counter-intuitive, evidence-based facts that separate real marketing from folklore.

## How brands actually grow (Ehrenberg-Bass / Byron Sharp)
- Growth comes mostly from **increasing penetration (reaching more light/occasional buyers)**, not from deepening
  loyalty of heavy buyers. Most of your sales come from many light buyers (the Pareto split is nearer 80/50 than 80/20).
- **Distinctiveness beats differentiation**: being noticed and easily recognised (brand assets) matters more than a
  rational point of difference, which buyers rarely perceive or care about.
- Build **mental availability** (come to mind at the buying moment, via many category entry points) and **physical
  availability** (easy to find/buy). "Loyalty programs drive growth" is largely a myth; they mostly reward existing behaviour.

## B2B specifics
- **The 95:5 rule**: at any time ~95% of business buyers are not in-market. Most advertising should build memory for
  the future 5%, not chase immediate conversion. Lead-gen-only strategies starve long-term growth.

## Measurement truths
- **Last-click attribution over-credits** the final touch (often branded search or a discount) and under-credits
  demand creation. Prefer incrementality tests (geo holdouts) and marketing-mix modelling.
- **Email open rates are unreliable** since Apple Mail Privacy Protection (2021) auto-opens — judge by clicks and
  conversions, not opens.
- A/B tests need pre-set sample size and one decision point — "peeking" and stopping at significance inflates false
  positives. Significance ≠ business impact.
- Case studies suffer survivorship bias; "they did X and grew" ignores everyone who did X and didn't.

## SEO reality
- **Keyword density is a myth**; stuffing hurts. Google rewards matching **search intent**, topical authority, and
  E-E-A-T (experience, expertise, authoritativeness, trust). Core Web Vitals are a minor ranking factor, not a silver bullet.

## Metrics, stated precisely
- CAC = total sales+marketing cost ÷ new customers. LTV ≈ ARPA × gross margin ÷ churn rate. LTV:CAC > 3 is a rough
  heuristic, meaningless without cohort data and CAC payback (< ~12 months).
