# Philosophy — specialist layer (precise distinctions and corrected misconceptions)

The exact distinctions and the popular misreadings models and laypeople repeat.

## Logic, stated precisely
- **Validity** is about form (if premises true, conclusion must follow); **soundness** = valid + actually-true premises.
  A valid argument can have a false conclusion if a premise is false. "Valid point" in everyday speech ≠ logical validity.
- Deduction guarantees; induction makes probable; abduction infers the best explanation.
- Named fallacies: affirming the consequent, denying the antecedent (formal); ad hominem, straw man, false dilemma,
  equivocation, appeal to authority/nature, slippery slope, begging the question (informal).

## Ethics distinctions
- **Hume's is–ought gap**: you can't derive a moral "ought" from purely factual "is" statements. The **naturalistic
  fallacy** (Moore) and the **appeal to nature** ("natural = good") are related errors.
- Metaethics (is morality objective?) ≠ normative ethics (what should I do?) ≠ applied ethics (specific cases).
  Consequentialism/utilitarianism, deontology (Kant), virtue ethics (Aristotle), contractualism (Rawls/Scanlon)
  answer the normative question differently; the trolley problem has **no single correct answer** — it reveals the
  clash between outcome-based and duty-based reasoning.

## Epistemology
- **Gettier problem**: justified true belief is *not sufficient* for knowledge — you can have a JTB that's true by luck.
  This broke the classical definition; responses add a "no false lemmas" or reliability condition.
- The **problem of induction** (Hume): no non-circular justification that the future resembles the past.

## Metaphysics (common survey positions)
- Free will: hard determinism, libertarianism, and **compatibilism** (free will is compatible with determinism) —
  compatibilism is the most common view among professional philosophers (PhilPapers survey), contrary to popular assumption.
- Mind: dualism vs physicalism/functionalism; the "hard problem" of consciousness is why there's subjective experience at all.

## Science
- **Popper's falsifiability** demarcates science (bold, testable, refutable claims); **Kuhn** describes paradigms and
  revolutions; neither says science is "just opinion". Underdetermination ≠ anything-goes.

## Misconceptions to reject
- "Philosophy is just opinions" — it's argument and analysis with standards. "Nihilism means anything goes" — it's a
  claim about meaning/values, not a permission slip. "Playing devil's advocate" ≠ a sound argument.
