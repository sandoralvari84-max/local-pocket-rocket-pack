# Product management — specialist layer (precise frameworks, corrected myths)

The exact definitions and the misused concepts that lead teams astray.

## MVP, correctly
- An MVP is the **smallest experiment that tests the riskiest assumption and delivers real learning** — not a cheap,
  broken v1. Ries's point is *validated learning*, not "minimum product". Shipping junk and calling it an MVP is the myth.
- Prototype fidelity matches the question: paper/clickable for usability; concierge or "Wizard of Oz" (humans behind
  the curtain) for demand; fake-door/landing page for interest.

## Product-market fit, measured
- **Retention curve flattening** (a stable cohort keeps using it) is the clearest PMF signal; a curve decaying to zero
  means no fit, no matter how many sign-ups. Sign-ups/downloads are vanity.
- **Sean Ellis test**: ≥ 40% of users would be "very disappointed" without the product is a common PMF benchmark.

## Prioritisation, exact
- **RICE** = (Reach × Impact × Confidence) ÷ Effort. **Kano**: basic (expected, dissatisfy if absent), performance
  (more is better), delight (unexpected) needs. **Cost of Delay / WSJF** (SAFe) = value ÷ job size.
- Beware the **build trap / feature factory**: measuring output (features shipped) instead of outcomes (behaviour change).

## Research truths
- Customers describe **problems and current behaviour** well; they're poor at specifying solutions or predicting their
  future behaviour. Ask about the last real time it happened, not hypotheticals. (The "faster horse" line is apocryphal,
  but the lesson holds: solve the job, don't take feature requests literally.)
- Continuous discovery (Teresa Torres): weekly customer contact, map opportunities before solutions, test the riskiest
  assumption first.

## Metrics
- **North Star** + input metrics; **AARRR** (acquisition, activation, retention, referral, revenue). Distinguish
  leading (predict) from lagging (confirm) indicators, and add guardrail metrics so a win in one doesn't break another.
- Experiment discipline: pre-register the hypothesis and primary metric; avoid peeking and HARKing.
