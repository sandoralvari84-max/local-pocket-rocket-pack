# Personal finance & investing — basics (mentor-curated seed, round 1)

General financial education on settled principles. NOT financial, investment or tax advice, and not personalised to your situation. For decisions about your money, consult a licensed professional.

## Budgeting & saving
- Spend less than you earn; pay yourself first (automate saving).
- A common guideline is the 50/30/20 split: needs / wants / savings & debt repayment.
- Build an emergency fund of roughly 3–6 months of essential expenses before investing.

## Debt & interest
- Compound interest grows balances (good for savings, costly for debt). Pay off high-interest debt (e.g. credit cards) first.
- Understand APR, minimum payments, and how interest capitalises.

## Investing principles (educational, not advice)
- Risk and expected return are linked; higher potential return means higher risk of loss.
- Diversification spreads risk across assets; don't concentrate in one holding.
- Time in the market and compounding matter; long horizons tolerate more volatility.
- Costs compound too: low-fee, broad index funds are a widely-taught default for beginners.
- Dollar-cost averaging (investing fixed amounts regularly) reduces timing risk.
- Only invest money you don't need short-term; be wary of hype, leverage and "guaranteed" returns.

## Risk management
- Insurance transfers large, unlikely risks (health, property, liability).
- Match asset risk to your time horizon and ability to tolerate loss.

## Accounting basics
- Assets = liabilities + equity (the accounting equation).
- Cash flow vs profit differ; track net worth (assets − liabilities) over time.

## Taxation (general — rules vary by country)
- Know which income is taxable, available allowances/deductions, and filing deadlines.
- Tax-advantaged accounts (e.g. pensions/retirement accounts) can aid long-term saving; specifics are jurisdictional.

# Finance & investing — advanced / academic layer (mentor-curated, round 2)

Builds on finance-basics.md. General financial education, NOT personalised investment, tax or financial advice. Consult a licensed professional for your own decisions.

## Time value of money
- Present value / future value; discounting; compounding frequency; annuities and perpetuities.
- Real vs nominal returns (adjust for inflation); the rule of 72 (years to double ≈ 72 ÷ %return).

## Portfolio theory
- Risk vs expected return; diversification reduces unsystematic (idiosyncratic) risk; systematic (market) risk remains.
- Modern Portfolio Theory (Markowitz): the efficient frontier; correlation between assets matters, not just individual risk.
- CAPM: expected return = risk-free + beta × equity risk premium; beta measures sensitivity to market moves.
- Asset classes: equities, bonds (duration, credit, rate sensitivity), cash, real assets; asset allocation drives most of long-run variance.

## Markets & valuation
- Efficient Market Hypothesis (weak/semi-strong/strong) — implications for active vs passive; most active managers underperform low-cost index funds net of fees over time.
- Valuation: discounted cash flow; multiples (P/E, EV/EBITDA); dividend discount model.

## Behavioural finance
- Biases: loss aversion, overconfidence, recency, herding, anchoring, mental accounting; they drive bubbles and panics.
- Discipline (rules, automation, rebalancing) beats market timing for most people.

## Risk, debt & planning (general principles)
- Match asset risk to time horizon and risk tolerance; emergency fund before investing; insure large tail risks.
- Compounding costs: fees and high-interest debt compound against you; pay down high-interest debt first.
- Tax-advantaged / retirement accounts aid long-term saving (specifics vary by country).
- Red flags: leverage you don't understand, "guaranteed" high returns, pressure, fraud (Ponzi/pump-and-dump).
