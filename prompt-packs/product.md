# Product research & management (mentor-curated seed, round 1)

## Discovery mindset
- Fall in love with the problem, not your solution. Validate demand before building.
- Continuous discovery: talk to customers weekly; pair discovery with delivery.
- Outcomes over outputs: measure the change in user/business behaviour, not features shipped.

## User interviews
- Recruit real target users; aim for ~5 per round to surface most issues, then iterate.
- Ask about past behaviour and specific recent events, not hypotheticals ("Tell me about the last time you…").
- Open questions, then stay silent; don't pitch or lead; separate what people say from what they do.
- Synthesise into patterns, jobs-to-be-done, and prioritised pains/needs.

## Competitive analysis
- Map direct, indirect and substitute competitors; compare positioning, features, pricing, and gaps.
- Find an angle rivals don't own; note their strengths honestly and where users are underserved.
- Watch switching costs and why users stay or leave.

## MVP strategy
- MVP = the smallest thing that tests the riskiest assumption and delivers real value.
- Prototype fidelity matches the question: paper/clickable for usability, concierge/"Wizard of Oz" for demand.
- Define a success metric and a kill criterion before you build.

## Prioritisation
- Frameworks: RICE (Reach, Impact, Confidence, Effort), MoSCoW, value-vs-effort matrix.
- Beware the build trap: shipping features nobody asked for. Tie each item to an outcome.

## Metrics
- Acquisition, activation, retention, referral, revenue (AARRR "pirate metrics").
- Retention is the clearest signal of product–market fit; a leaky bucket won't scale with more spend.

# Product management — advanced / academic layer (mentor-curated, round 2)

Builds on product-research.md.

## Strategy
- Product vision → strategy → roadmap → goals; strategy is choosing what NOT to do.
- OKRs: ambitious Objectives with measurable Key Results; outcomes (behaviour change) over outputs (features).
- Good Strategy/Bad Strategy (Rumelt): diagnosis → guiding policy → coherent actions; avoid "strategy" that is just goals.

## Continuous discovery (Teresa Torres)
- Opportunity Solution Tree: desired outcome → opportunities (needs/pains) → solutions → experiments.
- Weekly customer touchpoints; assumption mapping (desirability, viability, feasibility, usability); test the riskiest assumption first.
- Jobs-to-be-Done: people "hire" products for a job; focus on the progress they seek, not demographics.

## Prioritisation (depth)
- RICE (Reach × Impact × Confidence ÷ Effort); Kano model (basic/performance/delight needs); Cost of Delay and WSJF (weighted shortest job first); opportunity scoring (importance vs satisfaction).
- Beware the build trap and the feature factory; tie every bet to an outcome and a hypothesis.

## Metrics & experimentation
- North Star metric + input metrics; AARRR; leading vs lagging indicators; guardrail metrics.
- Retention cohorts and the retention curve flattening = the clearest product–market fit signal; Sean Ellis test (≥40% "very disappointed" without it).
- Experimentation: A/B with adequate power, one primary metric, pre-registered hypotheses; avoid peeking and HARKing.

## Growth & monetisation
- Growth loops vs funnels; activation "aha" moment; onboarding; network effects.
- Pricing & packaging: value metric alignment, tiering, willingness-to-pay research (Van Westendorp); expansion revenue and churn.

## Craft & influence
- Writing crisp PRDs/specs and problem statements; stakeholder alignment; influence without authority; saying no with rationale.
